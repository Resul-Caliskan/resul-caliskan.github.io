Information on the internet is more abundant than ever.

There are hundreds of articles, thousands of videos, and millions of lines of open-source code for almost any technology.

Yet, as time passed, I realized something:

> What is truly missing is not information, but **ways of thinking**.

When playing a game, everyone sees the same graphics. But some people ask *"Why is this mechanic engaging?"*

When using an app, most people only notice the feature. I ask *"Why was it designed this way?"*

When looking at an open-source project, before diving into the code, I try to grasp the core philosophy behind it.

This blog exists partly for that reason.

Rather than content like "How to use this React hook?" or "Which framework is best?", I want to share how I analyze a problem, the questions I ask, and how an idea matures over time.

Because in my view, great software engineering starts with asking the right questions before writing a single line of code.

## What will I write about?

This blog will not revolve around a single narrow topic.

Anything I am curious about, research, or spend time contemplating can find a spot here.

For instance:

- Game design and player psychology
- Software architecture & patterns
- Product development workflows
- Open-source projects
- Research methodologies
- Note-taking systems
- Artificial Intelligence tools
- Failed experiments
- Insights gained from books
- Daily engineering logs

Sometimes a single line from a book...

Sometimes a small idea discovered on GitHub...

And sometimes a question that has been lingering in my mind for days can be the catalyst for a new post.

## It doesn't have to be perfect

I used to wait for ideas to be fully complete before sharing them.

Then I realized that approach severely slowed down my growth.

That's why this blog won't be a polished showcase of perfect ideas.

It will be more like a living workspace.

Some posts won't contain definitive answers.

In some, my perspective will evolve over time.

And some will simply leave behind a great question.

And to me, that is the beauty of learning.

## Notes to my future self

In truth, the primary reader of this blog will be me.

Years from now, when I look back, I want to see not just the projects I built, but how I thought while building them.

> Code gets forgotten over time. But great questions remain.

Perhaps these notes will help someone else walking down a similar path.

Even if they don't, it will remain a living archive of my thoughts.
